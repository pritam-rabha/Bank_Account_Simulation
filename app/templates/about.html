Interest: simple or compound?

  • The calculator offers both. Simple: I = P×R×T÷100. Compound: A = P×(1+R÷(100n))^(nT).
  • Interest credited to accounts is always simple interest: savings interest on the current balance for 1–12 months chosen by the user (a simulated time step); fixed deposits receive simple interest for the whole term once, after maturity.
  • Assumptions: 12 equal months per year, constant rate, no tax or fees, final figure rounded half-up to 0.01.

Security model (educational)

  • PINs are hashed with salted scrypt; hashes are never displayed or returned by any page or API.
  • After repeated failures the account (and the client IP) is locked for a period; counters persist across restarts.
  • State-changing requests need a CSRF token; session cookies are signed, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax and expire after inactivity.
  • Changing the account number in a URL returns 403 Forbidden: you can only see your own account and records.
  • A short numeric PIN alone is not adequate protection for a real internet-facing financial service. Real systems add strong credentials, multi-factor authentication, device and risk checks, monitoring and regulatory compliance.

Limitations of JSON storage

  • The whole file is read and rewritten on each change — fine for a demo, not for scale.
  • Atomicity is provided by a lock plus an atomic file replacement, so a transfer is all-or-nothing on a single host. It does not offer a write-ahead log, replication or point-in-time recovery (only one .bak copy).
  • It assumes one machine with a normal local filesystem. Do not share the file over a network drive, and note that free cloud hosts (such as Render’s free tier) may erase local files on restart or redeploy.
  • For a real system a transactional database (for example PostgreSQL) is the right choice.
