How it works

  • Simple: I = P × R × T ÷ 100. Interest is always on the original principal.
  • Compound: A = P × (1 + R ÷ (100·n))^(n·T), I = A − P. Interest earns interest.
  • P principal, R annual rate %, T years (months ÷ 12), n compounding periods per year.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Educational model; not any real bank’s method or day-count convention. A year is 12 equal months.
  • Constant rate for the whole period; no tax, fees or penalties.
  • Calculated with Python Decimal; only the final figure is rounded (half-up) to 0.01.
  • Partial compounding periods use a fractional exponent — a modelling simplification.
  • An estimate is not a credit. Interest is added to an account only from the account’s Interest page, and then appears as an “Interest credited” transaction.
